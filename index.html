FREE Student-Led Reading Club — our programs

Welcome! iReading Club is one of our programs. Pick a group your child would love to join — tell us, and we will happily confirm your place. Places are limited, so come and join us soon!

Free means the student-led format: children read, think and create together by themselves — no teacher-led lessons.

Program 1 — iReading Club

Every Tuesday, 6:15-7:00 PM, online. First month free.

Tuesday Read-Aloud

Every Tuesday, 6:15-7:00 PM, online. We read aloud, read a short story, learn one small new thing, and create a storybook together.

Learn more

One child leads the read-aloud each week. Children take turns as host, questioner, recorder, summariser and timekeeper. Adults only keep time and ask one open question when the group gets stuck — we do not teach, give answers, or change children's stories.

Story Creation

Children create their own stories and storybooks together, in English, French or Chinese.

Learn more

We work on character motivation, cause and effect, problem-solving and character growth. First drafts can be imperfect — children revise because they want to, not because they are marked.

Magazine Team

Every so often we publish a magazine together. Each child gets a turn to be in charge of an issue, and parents can help.

Learn more

Our first magazine was made by six children. In the long term we hope to write a book together and give it to the library.

Monthly Offline Gathering

About once a month we meet in person: children play, discuss, and learn together like a study group.

Learn more

Place and date are announced in advance. Children research and present, and every child gets a chance to show leadership.

Program 2 — New program, coming soon

A second reading program

We are preparing another student-led reading program. You choose the time — pick the times that work for your child, and we group children with matching times together.

Learn more

Like iReading Club, the new program will be free to try and built around children reading, thinking and creating together — no exams, no rankings, no pressure. The schedule follows the children, not us: you pick your preferred times, and we put children with matching times in one group.

Leadership Academy

Every child has a strength — come and discover yours! In Leadership Academy, children learn to lead by actually leading — not by listening to lectures about leadership.

What children lead

Each child takes a turn, one step at a time:

Learn more

Lead a read-aloud. Host a story session. Lead a group discussion. Run a magazine issue. Present a topic to the group. Organise one activity at an offline gathering. Every role counts — a child who does not want to speak in front of everyone can lead in another way, for example by keeping time, recording ideas, or preparing materials.

How it works

Small steps, real practice, no exams and no rankings. Children prepare with support, lead for a short time, and get kind, specific feedback from the group.

Learn more

We rotate roles so every child gets a turn: host, questioner, recorder, summariser, timekeeper, presenter. Nobody is forced to perform. The goal is confidence and organisation ability — children learn to plan, to speak up, and to help others take part.

Start your own group or club

Got an idea for a group or club? Write it down clearly here — what you want to do, and who should join. We will help you find other children who like the same thing, set a time together, and do activities together.

Learn more

It works like this: (1) you write your idea; (2) we share it with other families to find members; (3) once enough children want to join, we set a time that works for everyone; (4) you lead the first activity, with our support. Your idea, your group — we just help you gather people and get started.

Student Tutors

Welcome! Older students tutor younger children — reading together, homework and math, one on one. It is wonderful for the younger child, and wonderful for the tutor too — come and join us!

Who can be a student tutor?

Students in Grade 5 and up, through the first three years of university. A tutor must be at least two grades above the student they teach.

Grade 5-6 students tutor Grades 1-3 in reading and simple homework. Grade 7-9 students tutor elementary school children. Grade 10-12 students tutor up to middle school. University students in years 1-3 tutor up to high school.

If you are under 18, your parent must agree before you can tutor.

First step: join the reading club

Before joining Student Tutors, every new student tutor first joins the Student-Led Reading Club as a tutor a few times — even one on one, just two children. This gives you experience, and lets us see how you teach. After that, you can move into Student Tutors.

What do student tutors teach?

Reading together, homework, math, English, French and Chinese reading, and study habits. For exams and IB diplomas, please see Find a Tutor for an adult teacher.

How it works

Students sign up to tutor, and parents ask for a student tutor. We check every student tutor first, then match you. You choose your own times, and we group children with matching times together. Lessons are online first. In-person lessons happen only with a parent present or in a public place — never alone, and never at the tutor's home.

Every match starts with a trial lesson, and the trial lesson is charged. A parent must be present for the trial lesson. You can stop after the trial if it is not a good fit.

Safety first

We check every student tutor before they start: a video chat, a short test, a reference, and a parent's confirmation. Online lessons use the parent's account or our link. We never show a student tutor's phone, email or other contact details on this website. We check in after the first three lessons, and every month after that. If there is any concern, we pause the match right away.

Student tutors cost less than adult teachers. Ask us for the price for your child.
